A request's `cost` may name an amount per limit — `{ rpm: 1, itpm: 1_200, otpm: 4_096 }` — for a client whose limits meter different things, as an AI API's requests and tokens per minute do. The object must name every limit the client meters, or the request fails with the new `RequestCostMismatchError`; a number is still charged to every limit.

A request may `settle` its cost against its response: it reserves an estimate when admitted, and `settle` reports what the response says was used, which the bucket is corrected to. A settlement never charges or credits the current window for an earlier one. A debit is reduced by the refill the bucket discarded at its ceiling since the claim, and that refill is forgiven once in total however many claims were outstanding while it was discarded, so overlapping settlements are each charged in full; a credit is dropped once the bucket has filled to its ceiling since the claim; and a settlement after a freeze, a reset, or the bucket expiring and being recreated applies nothing. A `requestLimit`'s new `maxDebt` lets a settlement take its balance below zero, and `requestOptions.settleWindow` bounds how late a settlement may arrive. A streamed response settles when its stream ends. A request with `settle` or `settleStream` on a backend that cannot settle is refused with `ConfigurationError` `backend_missing_settlement` before anything is spent.

A failed response's `retry-after` header is now the minimum for both the retry wait and the freeze. `retryOptions.classifyError` decides, ahead of the built-in rules, whether a failure is retried and whether it freezes the fleet, and may set a minimum freeze — so a spend-cap 429 can fail at once instead of freezing the fleet until the cap resets.

BREAKING: `getClientStats()` reports each request bucket's `cost` as a record keyed by limit name rather than as one number. A queued request's per-limit cost is stored as JSON, which a 1.x replica reads as a cost of 1 against every limit, under-charging a token limit; stop the fleet and restart it, as for the rest of 2.0. For a custom `DianemoBackend`: `acquireMultiLimit`, `releaseMultiLimit` and `tryAdmitMultiLimit` take the cost on each `MultiLimitSpec` rather than as an argument; `QueuedRequest.cost` is `number | Record<string, number>`; `getQueueStats` returns `costByLimit`; a token-bucket claim may carry a `ClaimMark`, recorded beside the bucket at `claimMarkKey(bucketKey, id)`; a bucket must keep an `epoch` naming its incarnation and a running total `forgiven` of the discarded refill settlements have used, and copy both into each mark it writes; and the new optional `settleTokens` and `emptyTokenBucket` back settlement. `RequestCostExceedsBudgetError` names the limit whose ceiling was exceeded when the client has several.
